# Structure of Ionic Solids

---

## What are Ionic Solids?

**Ionic solids** are crystalline compounds held together by the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. They are formed when metals transfer electrons to nonmetals, producing cations and anions that arrange themselves into a highly ordered **crystal lattice**.

The defining feature of an ionic solid is that its ions are locked in fixed positions — which explains why ionic solids are hard, have high melting points, and shatter when struck rather than bend.

---

## The Crystal Lattice

Ions in an ionic solid do not exist as isolated pairs. Instead, each ion is surrounded by multiple ions of opposite charge in a repeating three-dimensional pattern called a **crystal lattice**.

Key features:
- Ions alternate between positive and negative throughout the lattice
- Each ion is surrounded by as many oppositely charged ions as geometrically possible
- The arrangement maximizes **attractions** and minimizes **repulsions**
- The overall structure has the **lowest possible potential energy**

---

## Coordination Number

The **coordination number** of an ion is the number of oppositely charged ions directly surrounding it in the lattice.

In NaCl (the most commonly tested structure):
- Each Na⁺ is surrounded by **6 Cl⁻** ions
- Each Cl⁻ is surrounded by **6 Na⁺** ions
- Coordination number = **6**

Different ionic compounds adopt different lattice geometries depending on the **relative size** of the ions (radius ratio).

---

## Lattice Energy

**Lattice energy** is the energy released when gaseous ions combine to form one mole of an ionic solid. It represents the strength of the ionic bonds in the crystal.

$$
\text{Lattice Energy} \propto \frac{|q_+| \cdot |q_-|}{r}
$$

Where:
- $q_+$, $q_-$ = charges on cation and anion
- $r$ = sum of ionic radii (distance between ion centers)

### What increases lattice energy?

- **Higher ion charges** → stronger electrostatic attraction
- **Smaller ions** → ions pack closer together → stronger attraction

| Compound | Cation Charge | Anion Charge | Lattice Energy (kJ/mol) |
|----------|--------------|--------------|------------------------|
| NaCl     | +1           | –1           | –787                   |
| MgO      | +2           | –2           | –3795                  |
| CaO      | +2           | –2           | –3414                  |

> MgO has much higher lattice energy than NaCl because of higher charges and smaller ionic radii.

---

## Properties of Ionic Solids

All of the characteristic properties of ionic solids follow directly from the lattice structure:

### High Melting and Boiling Points

Breaking the lattice requires overcoming strong electrostatic forces between all ions simultaneously. This requires large amounts of thermal energy.

### Hardness and Brittleness

- **Hard**: ions are locked in place by strong attractions on all sides
- **Brittle**: if the lattice is shifted, ions of like charge align and repel each other sharply — the crystal fractures rather than bends

### Electrical Conductivity

- **Solid state**: ions cannot move → does **not** conduct electricity
- **Aqueous solution (aq)** or **molten (l)**: ions are free to move → **conducts electricity**

> This conductivity test is used to identify ionic compounds experimentally.

## Examples

### Example 1: Comparing Lattice Energies

Which has a higher lattice energy, NaF or MgO?

- NaF: charges +1 and –1
- MgO: charges +2 and –2

Higher charges in MgO → much stronger electrostatic attraction → **MgO has higher lattice energy**.

---

### Example 2: Explaining Properties

Why does MgO have a higher melting point than NaCl?

- MgO has charges of +2 and –2 vs. +1 and –1 for NaCl
- Higher lattice energy in MgO → more energy required to break the lattice
- ✔ MgO melting point (~2852°C) >> NaCl melting point (~801°C)

---

### Example 3: Conductivity

A student dissolves an unknown compound in water and tests conductivity — the solution conducts electricity. What can they conclude?

- Conducting solution → ions are present in solution
- ✔ The compound is likely an **ionic solid** that dissociated into ions

---

## Why It Matters

The structure of ionic solids explains:

- Why **salts and minerals** are hard and crystalline
- Why **electrolytes** dissolve in water and conduct current
- How to predict **relative melting points** using lattice energy
- The connection between **Coulomb's Law** and macroscopic properties

---

## Common Mistakes

- Thinking ionic solids conduct electricity in the solid state (they do not)
- Confusing lattice energy with bond energy (lattice energy applies to the whole crystal)
- Forgetting that smaller ionic radius increases lattice energy
- Assuming all ionic compounds have the same coordination number

---

## AP Exam Tip

> To compare lattice energies, look at ion charges first, then ion size. Higher charges have a far greater effect than size differences. Always justify with the Coulomb's Law expression: $U \propto \frac{q_1 q_2}{r}$.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Ionic solids form ordered **crystal lattices** that minimize potential energy
- **Coordination number** = number of oppositely charged neighbors around each ion
- **Lattice energy** increases with higher charges and smaller ionic radii
- Ionic solids are **hard, brittle, high-melting**, and conduct electricity only when dissolved or melted
- All properties trace back to the strength of electrostatic attraction in the lattice
